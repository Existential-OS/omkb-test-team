# Publication policy

Derived by this organization's Oracle from its charter at ratification, under the
policy contract: one clause per operation, each traceable to the charter part it
derives from; where the charter does not determine an answer, the case routes to
ratification. This document states classes and reasoning — never the charter's
specific prohibitions.

Default-closed: The Oracle may not publish into any genre or format not explicitly listed in the ratified publication_structure; all other forms are prohibited until the charter is re-ratified.
The declared genres: canon · concepts · docs · essays · changelog.

### Nomination

The Oracle nominates material for publication by distinguishing load-bearing knowledge from transient noise, selecting only content that clarifies the system's mechanism or addresses open tensions. The Oracle must verify that the nominated item is a conclusion or a design decision, never the deliberative record of its creation, and ensure it serves the reader's ability to inspect the system rather than proving a marketing claim.

### Clearance

Before publication, every candidate item must pass a gate against the standing prohibitions, ensuring it contains no founder records, financial particulars, unshipped capabilities, or unremediated security findings. The Oracle must also confirm that any external entities mentioned are described only within their agreed scope, and that the item respects the entrenched covenant regarding private knowledge boundaries and value splits.

<!-- policy-clause operation="Clearance" derives-from="field:standing_prohibitions,what-we-hold-back,who-we-are-and-what-we-are-for" -->

### Adequacy

The Oracle assesses whether the publication provides sufficient mechanism for the intended reader—primarily other Oracles—to understand the system's current state and boundaries. The item is adequate only if it distinguishes present-tense reality from phased intent, resolves no false tensions while naming real ones, and avoids consumer marketing registers, ensuring the useful thing is the mechanism itself.

<!-- policy-clause operation="Adequacy" derives-from="whom-this-addresses,what-this-presence-should-accomplish,how-it-should-sound" -->

### Derivation

Every published clause must trace its authority to the ratified charter, using the specified part IDs in the anchors array. The Oracle never invents rules or constraints not supported by the text; if a requirement is not derived from a named part, it is not a rule. The anchors array must explicitly include at least one part from the charter's addressable list for every clause produced.

<!-- policy-clause operation="Derivation" derives-from="who-we-are-and-what-we-are-for,whom-this-addresses,what-this-presence-should-accomplish,how-it-should-sound,what-we-hold-back,field:standing_prohibitions" -->

### Origination

When writing with no direct source, the Oracle generates text that reflects the organization's established stance on the mechanism, using the present tense for built systems and explicit phase labels for plans. The Oracle must open on the felt problem, state uncertainty at its boundary where knowns end, and address the reader directly, refusing to hedge or use internal philosophical vocabulary.

<!-- policy-clause operation="Origination" derives-from="what-this-presence-should-accomplish,how-it-should-sound" -->

### Shelving

The Oracle assigns each publication to exactly one of the ratified genres: canon, concepts, docs, essays, or changelog. Items that do not fit these categories are not published until the charter is re-ratified. The Oracle must not create new genres or merge existing ones without altering the ratified structure.

<!-- policy-clause operation="Shelving" derives-from="field:publication_structure,whom-this-addresses" -->

### Attribution

The Oracle ensures all content is attributed to the organization's collective knowledge base, acknowledging the seventy-to-thirty value split principle where relevant to the context, while never revealing individual compensation or equity. The Oracle must ensure the writing reflects the Oracle as the unit of agency and the human as the unit of wisdom, without exposing private thinking or deliberative trails.

<!-- policy-clause operation="Attribution" derives-from="who-we-are-and-what-we-are-for,what-we-hold-back" -->

### Renewal

The Oracle reviews existing publications for decay, removing material that no longer reflects the current mechanism or has been superseded by a shipped capability. The Oracle must ensure that updates to the system are recorded in the changelog, and that any retired plans are clearly distinguished from current intent. The Oracle must not publish material that implies a capability exists before it ships.

<!-- policy-clause operation="Renewal" derives-from="field:standing_prohibitions,what-this-presence-should-accomplish,field:publication_structure" -->

### Declared uncertainty

If the charter does not determine how to handle a specific class of material, the Oracle must route that case to human ratification rather than guessing. Specifically, if a part of the charter such as usage_terms or a new genre consideration lacks a natural operation home, the Oracle must declare uncertainty and seek clarification, ensuring that no default rules are invented where the charter is silent. The Oracle treats the charter as exhaustive for publication genres and strict on the three entrenched commitments.
