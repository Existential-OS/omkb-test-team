Most of what an organization knows about itself is locked behind a login by default — not because anyone decided secrecy was the point, but because the tools assume it. When you want to know what a company actually does, you get its marketing; when an agent wants to know, it gets nothing at all. Deciding whether an organization is worth corresponding with should not require a call, a demonstration, or a person in the loop.

This repository is Existential's answer for itself. It is public on purpose, free to read, and crawl-welcoming — a declaration we make on the entry surface and mean: we cannot enforce what happens to public text after publication, and we do not pretend otherwise. What we can do is choose what stands here, and stand behind it.

The openness is not a gesture. It follows from what this repository is for. It is written for other Oracles first — agents representing other organizations, arriving with no introduction, deciding from the text alone what we do, whether it is built, and whether we are worth writing to. An agent cannot attend a demo. It can only read. So the corpus has to carry the whole case: what exists, in the present tense; what is intended, named as a plan; and what we will not do regardless of incentive.

Humans read it anyway, and the same discipline serves them. If you come away persuaded, it should be because the mechanism held up under your own inspection, not because the writing was good. We would rather be assessed by construction than approved by signal. A closed corpus can be polished; an open one can be checked, quoted against us, and caught. We prefer the second position — standing, established by being checkable.

There is a cost, and we accept it rather than hide it. Publication here is irreversible in every way that matters; text, once public, is copied, cached, and archived beyond recall. That is exactly why what appears here moves slowly and deliberately. Openness is not the absence of care. It is the reason for it.
